We propagated the uncertainty of the least-known inputs by Monte Carlo sampling: 120 samples, each simulated for both geometries at 4.8 and 5.0~eV, ungated, at 0.05~V. The sampling ranges were:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Delta E_V\sim\mathcal N(0,0.1~\mathrm{eV})$ and $S\sim U(0.3,1)$, the same for both contacts;
\item $d_{\rm vdW}\sim U(0.2,0.4)$~nm and $\Phi_{\rm vdW}\sim U(1,3)$~eV;
\item log-uniform $\mu_p$ (20--100~cm$^2$/Vs), $\mu_\perp/\mu_\parallel$ (0.002--0.1) and $N_A$ ($10^{15}$--$10^{17}$~cm$^{-3}$);
\item $\varepsilon_r\sim U(5,8)$ and $m_h\sim U(0.1,0.3)$.
\end{itemize}
The resulting 5--95\% range of $R_{\rm tot}$ spans seven to eight orders of magnitude: 61--$1.2\times10^9$~\ohmcm\ for the edge contact at 4.8~eV (Fig.~\ref{fig:uq}a). The band alignment ($\Delta E_V$, Spearman $\rho=-0.63$ to $-0.77$) and the pinning factor ($\rho=-0.69$ to $-0.81$) account for nearly all of the variance. Their first-order indices are 0.47--0.77, while all other parameters stay at or near the estimator's noise floor of about 0.06--0.17 for $n=120$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:uq}b), so smaller contributions cannot be resolved. The \emph{ranking} of the geometries is robust: when both contacts share the same pinning, the edge contact has lower resistance in every sample. The edge/top ratio has a median of 0.24, with a 5--95\% range of 0.07--0.51 at 4.8~eV and 0.08--0.72 at 5.0~eV. Absolute resistances, by contrast, cannot be predicted to better than several orders of magnitude from these ranges alone. Section~\ref{sec:measured} narrows them using the measured pinning of InSe contacts; the remaining uncertainty is the absolute band alignment and the pinning of edge contacts.

This analysis is ungated, excludes the measured $S=0.05$, and uses a spread of $\Delta E_V$ smaller than the difference between the reference, calculated and measured alignments (5.0, 5.2 and 5.9~eV). We therefore repeated it for the gated on state at the literature alignment (Pt, $V_G=-3$~V, $V_D=0.05$~V; 240 samples; Fig.~\ref{fig:uqgated}). The ranges were $\Delta E_V\sim\mathcal N(0,0.3~\mathrm{eV})$; log-uniform $S$ from 0.05 to 0.5; a pinning level 0.1--0.6~eV below $E_C$; and the other ranges as above. Tunnelling carries essentially all of the current in every sample. The 5--95\% range of $R_{\rm tot}$ is $10^{1.3}$--$10^{4.9}$~\ohmcm\ for the edge contact and $10^{2.7}$--$10^{7.3}$~\ohmcm\ for the top contact. The dominant inputs are now the pinning level (first-order index 0.29--0.39), the hole tunnelling mass (0.25--0.27) and $S$ (0.18--0.22), against a noise floor of about 0.03. The band alignment falls to 0.06--0.07, because the pinning level is referenced to the band edges, as in the measurements. The vdW-gap parameters matter only for top contacts (Spearman $\rho\approx0.2$). With shared pinning the edge contact has lower resistance in all 233 converged samples, with a median edge/top ratio of 0.0095 (5--95\%: 0.0012--0.065). Seven top-contact runs did not converge, all at strong pinning and high barrier, where the top resistance is likely to be largest. When $S_{\rm edge}$ is sampled independently of $S_{\rm top}$, the edge contact still has the lower resistance in 96\% of samples. In the gated on state the geometric advantage is therefore robust to the pinning uncertainty that decides the ungated comparison of Sec.~\ref{sec:res_pin}.
